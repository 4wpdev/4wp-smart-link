What Is Included in a Solar Installation Price?

A professional quotation typically covers the solar panels, inverter, mounting system, electrical protections, cabling, installation labour, system configuration, monitoring, technical documentation and legalisation. Depending on the property, it may also include battery storage, EPS/backup provisions, electrical modifications, specialised mounting hardware, additional protection devices, or longer cable runs where the electrical room is far from the roof.

Two quotations with the same number of panels are not necessarily equivalent. The brand and tier of the inverter, the quality of the mounting system, the scope of electrical work included, and whether legalisation and documentation are covered can all vary significantly between installers.

What Determines the Cost of a Solar Installation?

Solar Panels

Panel cost is influenced by power output, efficiency, build quality and how efficiently a panel uses the available roof space. Higher-efficiency panels can generate more electricity per square metre, which matters on smaller or partially shaded roofs, but efficiency and price should be judged specification by specification rather than assumed.

Inverter

A grid-connected (string) inverter is generally the simplest and most economical option for solar-only systems. A hybrid inverter is designed to work with a battery and typically includes system monitoring, and in many cases is a prerequisite for adding storage later. A hybrid inverter alone does not automatically provide backup power during a grid outage.

Battery Storage

Adding a battery increases the upfront investment but allows more of the electricity generated during the day to be used later, rather than exported. This can increase self-consumption and support greater energy independence. Backup during a grid outage is only possible with a compatible inverter, the battery itself, and a correctly configured EPS/backup setup — a battery and hybrid inverter alone are not automatically sufficient.

Roof Type and Installation Complexity

Flat concrete roofs, tiled roofs and metal roofs each require a different mounting approach, and no single method suits every property. Orientation, access, waterproofing, wind exposure, and the distance between the roof and the electrical room all affect both the installation approach and its cost.

How Does a Home Solar System Work?

Solar panels generate direct current (DC) electricity from sunlight. The inverter converts this into alternating current (AC), which the home uses directly. Where a battery is installed, any surplus generated beyond the home's immediate needs can be stored for later use, and any remaining surplus may be exported to the grid.

What Happens to the Solar Energy You Don't Use?

Electricity your panels generate but the home doesn't use immediately can go to two places: it can be stored in a battery for later use, or exported to the grid. In Spain, under the simplified self-consumption compensation mechanism established by Real Decreto 244/2019, qualifying self-consumption installations may receive compensation against their electricity bill for surplus exported to the grid. This is a bill credit under a simplified compensation scheme rather than a direct sale of electricity, and whether it applies — and on what terms — depends on the property's self-consumption arrangement and electricity contract.

The objective of a well-designed system is not simply to install the maximum possible number of panels — it is to design a system that produces electricity the property can actually use economically.

Solar Installation Permits and Legalisation in Andalucía

A professional solar installation includes not only installing the equipment but also the technical and administrative steps required to correctly legalise the system. Every grid-connected solar installation in Spain must comply with applicable electrical and self-consumption regulations, which generally involves preparing technical documentation and formally registering the installation with the relevant authorities.

Part of this process involves the CAU (Código de Autoconsumo), an official code that links your installation to your electricity supply point so that grid arrangements and any applicable compensation can be applied correctly. The exact procedure can vary depending on the property, system size and connection type, so it should not be assumed to be identical for every installation. How many solar panels do I need for 5 kW?

A 5 kW system using 500 W modules requires approximately 10 panels. The exact number can vary slightly depending on the wattage of the specific panel model chosen.

Is a solar battery worth installing?

It depends on your consumption profile. A battery increases self-consumption and can support backup, but it also adds upfront cost. Properties with high evening or night-time consumption, or a desire for backup power, often benefit more from a battery than those with mostly daytime usage.

Can I receive compensation for excess solar electricity?

Under Spain's simplified self-consumption compensation framework, surplus electricity exported to the grid can, in many cases, generate a bill credit rather than a direct cash payment. Eligibility and terms depend on your electricity contract and self-consumption arrangement.

Do solar panels work during a power cut?

Not automatically. Standard grid-connected solar systems shut down during a power cut for safety reasons. Backup during an outage requires a compatible hybrid inverter, a battery, and a correctly configured EPS/backup setup — not simply having a battery installed.
